Frontrow · assessment · 12 November 2026

Why people do not come back, and how the Clubhouse can fix it

The consultant said the journey is make or break for return visits, and that the product should be a social platform built around direct bets. This page tests both against the live product. Part one covers the user journey and the bets. Part two covers the Clubhouse: what to improve in its look, its experience, and what to build on top. The work items are in BACKLOG.md (J-01 to J-21 and C-01 to C-18).

Return 7 of 62

people used Frontrow in the last 7 days. 51 of the 62 accounts were created in June for the World Cup, and most have not been seen since. Nobody has signed up since 7 September.

Direct bets 0 settled, ever

Two bets between friends have been created in production. Neither finished. The feature is built, but it has never paid out once, so it is not proven to work.

First five minutes 6 steps, 0 on bets

The welcome sequence introduces a football fantasy game under the old product name. Friends, coins and bets are never mentioned, and it ends on a screen that is empty for most newcomers.

The consultant is right on both points, and they are the same point. The social layer and the bets are the reason to open the app between tournaments, and today a new person cannot reach them without already understanding how the product is organised. The fix is mostly about order and wording, with a small amount of repair work on the bets themselves.

Are direct bets up and running?

Built, partly switched on, never completed

The answer is no, not yet. All the pieces are there, but no bet between friends has ever run from creation to payout in the live product, and one of the two types has its automation turned off.

PieceState todayEvidence
One-to-one challenge Stuck
One created on 30 August. Never accepted. It was due to expire on 1 September and is still open, with the creator's 100 coins held.		 The background jobs that expire and auto-settle challenges are switched off and have no run history.

One created on 3 September with a 250-coin stake. Two people joined. It closed the next day and nobody declared a winner.		 Its three background jobs are on and ran successfully, but they cannot settle a bet no one declares.

62 welcome grants, 3 stakes, 0 payouts, 0 refunds.		 Every coin ever staked is still held. Buying coins is marked "Coming Soon".

Never tested by two real accounts in a browser.		 The project's own tracker says so on each bets release.

None of this is a large build. It is one supervised test with two accounts, three switches, and a decision about what happens when nobody declares a result. It should happen before anyone new is invited, because a first bet that hangs forever is worse than no bets at all.

The journey as it is today

Seven moments, walked at phone size

Each stage lists what a new person meets and how it was checked. "Live site" means the public address. "Walk-through" means the app run locally at 375px wide. "Code" means read in the source.

Moment 1

Arriving

Weak
  • The first screen is a login form. A logo, "Sign In" and "Create Account". Nothing says what Frontrow is or why a friend sent the link.
  • The best one-line description in the product, Every game you play lives in one room, only appears after an account exists.
Live site
Moment 2

Signing up

Friction
  • Four fields before anything happens: username, email, password, confirm password.
  • No Google or Apple sign-in and no email link. For a product people join because a friend nudged them on a phone, each field costs sign-ups.
Live site
Moment 3

The welcome sequence

Wrong product
  • It introduces a different product. The second card reads Welcome to Forza Fantasy League and describes a football fantasy league. F1, tennis, coins, bets and chat are never mentioned.
  • The step counts disagree. One card shows "Step 1 of 4" and "4 / 6" at the same time.
  • It asks for a username again, straight after sign-up asked for it.
  • It quotes a $100M budget. The Market shows €100M.
  • A wrong invite code fails silently. If the code is mistyped or the Clubhouse cannot be created, the sequence moves on as if it worked. This is one possible reason 12 people ended up in no Clubhouse.
  • On a phone it shows the hint "Press → to advance".
Walk-throughCode
Moment 4

The first real screen

Dead end
  • The sequence ends by opening the Market. For anyone not yet in a football league it shows squad 0/15, budget €0.0M and rows that load forever.
  • Someone who came for F1, tennis or a bet with a friend lands in a football transfer screen with nothing they can do.
Walk-through
Moment 5

Home

No pull
  • Home is a directory, not a feed. It lists the Clubhouses and competitions you belong to. It does not say what happened since your last visit or what is waiting on you.
  • When empty, Home and the Club tab show almost the same three options, so two of the five bottom tabs look identical to a newcomer.
  • Inside a Clubhouse, bets are one small tile labelled "P2P Challenges", next to the coin wallet.
Walk-throughCode
Moment 6

Making a first bet

Buried
  • Two tabs for one idea. "Bets" is a read-only list. To create a bet you must go to "Coins". Nothing on the Bets tab says so.
  • Six names for the same thing: Bets, Coins, Coin challenges, P2P Betting, Challenge, Group bet, plus "Freeform".
  • Without a Clubhouse the create window says to join one first and offers no button to do it.
  • On a phone, the wallet, a season record and a long explainer sit above the list of actual bets. The explainer uses the words "escrow" and "5% rake burned".
  • The form is long and the ending is longer. A group bet asks for question, answer type, options, who can join, stake, start and end. Settling it can pass through nine states, including an objection window and arbitration.
Walk-throughCode
Moment 7

Coming back

Nothing calls
  • There is no channel outside the app. No push notification and no email when a friend challenges you, a bet settles or a race weekend starts.
  • The automated newspaper produces most notifications (294 of 375). Five in six of all notifications are never opened.
  • Between tournaments there is nothing new to do. Bets on anything are the natural filler, and they are the hardest thing in the app to find.

What is already working

The parts worth keeping

  • The Clubhouse idea is sound. One room for a group of friends, with every sport inside it, is exactly the social structure the consultant described. 81% of sign-ups got into one.
  • A core group returns without being asked. 17 people were active in the last month with no reminders of any kind.
  • People do bet when it is in front of them. 16 placed league prediction bets and 6 placed F1 race bets, both shown inside the competition they were already using.
  • The breadth is real. Football, F1 and tennis all run. The problem is that a newcomer meets all of it at once.

The journey to aim for

Start with a bet from a friend

Make the invitation itself the product. A person should meet a real bet from someone they know before they meet a form, a wizard or a squad builder.

  1. A friend sends a bet

    The link opens a page that shows the bet: who is asking, the question, the stake. No login wall in front of it.

  2. One tap to join

    Sign in with Google, Apple or an email link. Joining puts you in that friend's Clubhouse automatically.

  3. Take the bet

    One line explains the 500 free coins. One button accepts. The first minute ends with something at stake.

  4. Home shows your move

    Bets waiting on you, results, what friends did. Fantasy competitions appear as the next thing to try, not the entry fee.

This keeps everything already built. It changes which door people come through and what they see first.

Part two · the Clubhouse

The Clubhouse is a lobby. It needs to be the room.

Today the Clubhouse home shows three shortcuts, a grid of competitions and a feed of league news. Each of them sends you somewhere else to act. The social part, chat, sits in a separate tab and has almost never been used. The idea behind everything below: what friends do together (bet, talk, react, gloat) should happen inside the room, in one stream, without leaving.

What to improve in the look

Checked against the ui-ux-pro-max rule set. The first two are faults, not taste.

C-01

Chat faults on a phone S

Deleting a message only appears on mouse hover, so it cannot be done on a touch screen. A message that fails to send shows nothing. A sender's name can show as "?" because of a small logic slip.

C-02

Invite still says the old name S

The WhatsApp invite reads "Join my Clubhouse on Forza Fantasy League". Rename to Frontrow and use the phone's own share sheet so it works with any app.

C-03

Six tabs down to three M

Home, The Frontrow, Digest, Chat, Members, Settings becomes Room, Competitions, People. Settings moves behind a gear for the owner. The newspaper becomes a card in the Room.

C-04

One "your move" line instead of three shortcut cards S

Wallet, challenges and rank are three exits in a sideways scroller, the first thing you see. Replace with one line that only appears when something waits for you, and a small coin balance in the header.

C-05

People, not a list of names M

Members shows a letter, a join date and a trophy count. Give each person a record and one button: Challenge.

C-06

Tidy-up S

Emoji used as icons replaced with the app's icon set; hard-coded colours moved to design tokens; chat height fixed for phone browsers where the address bar moves.

What to improve in the experience

C-07

One room, one stream M

Merge chat and activity into a single feed. Drop the Slack layout (channels, "select a channel", a DMs tab): a group of friends has one conversation. Keep the data, hide the structure.

C-08

Bets happen in the room Mapproval

The bet button in chat currently navigates away, and a bet card in chat can only be viewed, not accepted. Make "+ Bet" open a short sheet over the chat, and let friends take or pass on the card itself. Depends on J-01 to J-03.

C-09

The feed is about people Mapproval

Activity lists league events only. Add: someone joined, a bet was made, taken, won; a new newspaper edition.

C-10

Reactions on everything Mapproval

The cheapest social act there is, and it is missing from chat and from the feed. One tap on a result or a lost bet is how a quiet room starts talking.

C-11

An empty room that tells you what to do S

A new Clubhouse offers one button: create a competition. Offer three instead: invite, make the first bet, say hello.

C-12

Notifications about friends, landing in the right place Mapproval

Notify on "Rui challenged you", "you won 50", "Ana replied", and open that exact item in the Room instead of a general bets page.

What to build on top

Each gives the group a reason to come back that does not depend on a fantasy deadline.

C-13

Rivalries Mapproval

A head-to-head record between any two members: bets won and lost, fantasy meetings, coins exchanged. "You are 2–5 against Rui" is the most re-shareable line the product can produce.

C-14

The Frontrow as a weekly ritual about the group Mapproval

The newspaper is the most distinctive thing here and nobody reacts to it. Make it weekly and about the people (bet of the week, worst call, biggest climber), post it into the Room, and make it shareable as an image.

C-15

Match-day prompts Mapproval

Before a big fixture, race or final, the Room gets a ready-made question: "Benfica v Porto, Saturday. Who's calling it?" One tap to pick, one more to put coins on it. Delivers J-17 where the friends are.

C-16

Free picks as the on-ramp to bets Mapproval

A one-tap prediction with no coins at stake. Lower commitment than a bet, and it shows who agreed with whom.

C-17

Season ledger and awards Mapproval

A running table of who is up and down in coins inside this Clubhouse, with end-of-season awards feeding the Trophy cabinet.

C-18

Share cards M

A settled bet, a rivalry record or a newspaper front page as an image for WhatsApp, with a link back. Same door as the bet invite (J-10): the content is the invitation.

S is about a day, M a few days. "Approval" means a database or server change that will be named and confirmed before it runs.

Recommended order of work

Repair, then simplify, then grow

One dependency rules the rest: the bet invite (J-10) and bets in the room (C-08) both need a bet that completes end to end, so J-01 to J-03 stay first. The small Clubhouse fixes C-01, C-02 and C-11 have no dependencies and can go alongside. The Room redesign (C-07 to C-09, with C-03 and C-04) is one piece of work and belongs in the second tier.

Now · days

Repair what is broken

  1. Prove one bet end to end with two test accounts, then switch the paused bet jobs back on and release the three held stakes. Each needs your approval.
  2. Fix the welcome sequence: correct name, one step count, one currency, show an error on a bad invite code, drop the second username step, end on Home.
  3. Call it "Bets" everywhere and merge the Bets and Coins tabs into one.
  4. Record the funnel: sign-up, Clubhouse joined, first bet, return on day 1, 7 and 30.
  5. Clubhouse quick fixes: chat on a phone, invite wording, a helpful empty room (C-01, C-02, C-11).
Next · 2 to 4 weeks

Simplify the path

  1. Bet invite link with its own landing page, as described above.
  2. Three-step welcome: your name, your Clubhouse, your first bet or sport.
  3. Quick bet: question, stake, who. Everything else behind "More options".
  4. Lighter settlement for friendly bets: the creator declares, others have 24 hours to object, then it pays out by itself.
  5. Home as a feed with "your move" at the top.
  6. One-tap sign-in.
  7. The Room: one stream of chat, bets and results, three tabs, bets taken in place, reactions and friend notifications (C-03 to C-10, C-12).
Later · structural

Give people a reason to return

  1. A channel outside the app. Email first because it is cheap, push once the mobile app ships.
  2. A public front page that says what Frontrow is.
  3. Fewer words. Clubhouse, Competition, Bets, Coins. Fold the rest into those.
  4. Ready-made bets per event for a race, a match or a tennis final, so there is something to do every week, not only during a World Cup.
  5. Built on the Room: weekly Frontrow, rivalries, free picks, season ledger, share cards (C-13 to C-18).

Limits of this assessment

  • The populated Clubhouse could not be opened in the local demo, so part two rests on the code and on database counts, plus a phone-size look at the empty lobby.
  • No account was created on the live site. Signed-in screens were walked in the local demo build, where bet actions are blocked, so the bet forms were read in code and not submitted.
  • No two-account bet test was run. That is the first recommended action.
  • 62 accounts is a friends-and-family pilot. The pattern is clear; the exact percentages would move with a larger group.
  • Nothing in the product or database was changed to produce this page. All database access was read-only.
